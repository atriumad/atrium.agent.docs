# *Colors*: four values carry the brand

**Charcoal**, **cream**, **lime** and **clay**. Sage is carried for texture-adjacent surfaces. There is no black and no pure white. Every value below lives in `src/design/tokens.ts`, and the CSS custom properties are generated from it.

## Using them

The default pairing is **charcoal on cream**. The inverse, **cream on charcoal**, carries proof bands, footers and deck openers. Lime and clay are punctuation.

| Color | Share of a screen | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cream | About 75% | The page, cards, fields, tables |
| Charcoal | About 20% | Text, rules, the primary button, dark fields |
| Lime | 4% or less | One call to action, one marker, one card |
| Clay | 1% or less | Focus, errors, a quote, a count that needs a person |

## Status colors

The system has one signal and one warm color, and no green, amber or blue. A dashboard still has to say "done" and "late", so status is told apart by **fill, a 3 px left rule and a mark**, never by hue alone. This is the product layer, built only from the palette.

:::caution Color never speaks alone
Every status has a word and a mark. Lime reads as positive and clay as late, but a person who cannot tell them apart still reads "Overdue" and sees the left rule and the icon. Text on a status fill always passes 4.5:1; see [Accessibility](./accessibility).
:::

## Data colors

For charts, in the order series are used. Six is the limit: beyond that, group or use small multiples. Adjacent series never rely on hue alone, so charts also use position, labels or direct annotation.

## The dark theme

Dark is a charcoal field, not an inversion. The roles are redefined: the page is `charcoal-900`, cards are `charcoal-800`, figures and the primary action turn lime, and status colors lighten to hold contrast.

## Do and don't

<DoDontRow>
  <DoDont kind="do">Use charcoal on cream, and cream on charcoal. Both clear 11:1.</DoDont>
  <DoDont kind="dont">Use clay-500 or lime-600 as text on cream. They fail contrast.</DoDont>
  <DoDont kind="do">Put <Highlight>one</Highlight> lime marker behind the word that matters.</DoDont>
  <DoDont kind="dont">Use clay as the main button, or put two lime fields in one view.</DoDont>
</DoDontRow>
